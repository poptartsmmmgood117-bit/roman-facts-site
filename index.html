IV · Annales

Rise & Fall

Two thousand years, from legend to the last emperor.

753 BC

Founding

By legend, Romulus founds Rome after killing his twin brother Remus. The two had been raised by a she-wolf.

509 BC

The Republic

Rome throws out its last king and becomes a republic led by two elected consuls. For centuries afterwards, Romans hated the word "king".

218 – 201 BC

Hannibal

Carthage's greatest general marches elephants over the Alps and destroys Roman armies, but Scipio finally beats him at Zama.

146 BC

Carthage Burns

Rome razes Carthage and the same year destroys Corinth. It now dominates the Mediterranean.

44 BC

The Ides of March

Julius Caesar, dictator for life, is stabbed 23 times by senators. The Republic dies with him.

27 BC

Augustus

Caesar's adopted heir becomes the first emperor. Two centuries of relative peace, the Pax Romana, begin.

AD 79

Vesuvius

The volcano buries Pompeii and Herculaneum, preserving a Roman town down to the bread in its ovens.

AD 117

Greatest Extent

Under Trajan, the empire stretches from Britain to Mesopotamia.

AD 476

Fall of the West

The Germanic general Odoacer deposes the teenage emperor Romulus Augustulus. The Western Empire is gone.

AD 1453

The Last Romans

The Eastern Empire, ruled from Constantinople, survives another thousand years until the Ottomans take the city.

V · Corrections

Myth vs. Fact

What the movies taught you, and what actually happened.

Myth

Thumbs down meant death in the arena.

Fact

Nobody knows which gesture meant what. The famous thumbs-down image comes from an 1872 painting by Jean-Léon Gérôme.

Myth

Romans had vomitoriums to throw up during feasts.

Fact

A vomitorium was an exit passage in an amphitheater that "spewed out" the crowd.

Myth

Nero fiddled while Rome burned.

Fact

The fiddle wouldn't be invented for another thousand years, and Tacitus says Nero was at Antium when the fire broke out.

Myth

Gladiators always fought to the death.

Fact

Gladiators were expensive to train. Many fights ended with a decision, and most fighters survived most bouts.

Myth

Roman statues and temples were gleaming white.

Fact

They were painted in bright colors. Most of the paint has worn away, but traces survive.

Myth

Rome fell in 476.

Fact

Only the West did. The Eastern Roman Empire lasted until 1453, and its people called themselves Romans the whole time.
